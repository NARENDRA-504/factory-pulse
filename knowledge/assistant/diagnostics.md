# Temperature and cooling investigation
Machine temperature, motor temperature and coolant temperature are separate channels. Compare the exact sensor and unit with its recorded configured range, controller alarm, operating mode and timestamp. A cooling issue, load change or sensing problem is only an investigation candidate until verified. Coolant flow is in L/min and cannot be substituted for a temperature measurement. Record any visible external leakage only from a safe observation point; do not open hot or pressurized circuits or recommend fluid changes from this guidance. Use the retrieved role-specific SOP for escalation and authorized inspection. This is training guidance, not a diagnosis or an approved operating procedure.

# Vibration and spindle-speed investigation
Compare vibration in its actual unit with records from comparable operating modes and spindle speeds. Velocity in mm/s and acceleration in m/s² are different measurements and cannot share a numeric limit without an approved conversion and specification. Record job/tool changes and controller alarms. Do not infer bearing damage or imbalance solely from one point. Do not recommend raising spindle speed, reaching into the guarded area or bypassing an interlock to reproduce an alarm. Have the authorized team use its equipment inspection plan.

# Pressure and electrical investigation
Pressure in bar, current in A and voltage in V are different evidence channels. Missing values, wrong units or old timestamps cannot establish current machine condition. A pressure excursion does not identify a failed pump or valve; an electrical excursion does not identify a failed motor or drive. Preserve the controller alarm and reading IDs for the authorized team. Keep electrical panels closed and do not loosen pressure fittings or repeatedly reset protective trips. Equipment isolation and inspection require the applicable approved procedure and authorized personnel.

# Evidence before a recommendation
Separate observations, configured comparisons, hypotheses and approved actions. A fresh numeric GOOD sample with the correct unit can support a current comparison, but configured bounds are not necessarily manufacturer-approved limits. Multiple sensors can share a round-wide status; find the individual sensor's range comparison before attributing a cause. No model output, score or cleared chart authorizes a physical repair or restart. If the records are stale, describe them as last known and request verified current evidence; do not invent readings, persistence duration or safety approval.

# Response ownership and restart verification
An Operator captures machine/sensor/time/alarm evidence and reports to the assigned supervisor and maintenance team. The Supervisor prioritizes, assigns authorized review and coordinates coverage. Maintenance records actual findings, work performed and verification through the approved equipment procedure. Managers coordinate major-risk recovery and check verified operational impact. Admins investigate authorized data/access configuration issues. Incident closure and SOP acknowledgement are records of workflow, not physical release authorization. A return to normal after an incident does not remove the site's required release and restart checks.

# Guidance approval boundary
FactoryPulse's built-in SOP documents are training drafts. An approved equipment procedure needs the correct machine mapping, a real approving owner, manual reference and effective/review dates. General hazardous-energy reference: https://www.osha.gov/control-hazardous-energy/ . This reference does not approve a local procedure, establish local legal compliance or provide an equipment-specific isolation sequence. Never invent approval metadata or an isolation sequence.
